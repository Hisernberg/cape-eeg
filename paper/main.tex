\begin{abstract}
Continuous EEG monitoring in intensive care produces expert labels that disagree, and next-generation clinical systems must estimate the \emph{distribution} of expert opinion on small, auditable hardware. We present CAPE-EEG, a pre-registered study of whether a 164k-parameter two-view convolutional model can match a small ImageNet-pretrained network when both consume the same byte-capped evidence. Each labelled 10-second window is represented by a \emph{foveated} log-spectrogram of the surrounding 50-second raw EEG (half of the 32 time bins on the labelled centre) and a 64$\times$64 summary of the supplied 10-minute spectrogram, stored as float16 with bit-packed validity masks in a 5.58\,GB cache. The candidate fuses the two views with a learned gate and predicts observed annotator disagreement as an auxiliary target. On 106,800 windows from 1,950 patients of the HMS Harmful Brain Activity dataset we froze a patient-disjoint 60/10/5/5/20 split, capped development at eight named configurations, locked the comparator, schedule and referral score before any test access, and evaluated once. The candidate is \emph{inferior}: three-seed patient-mean KL divergence is 0.934 versus 0.869 for MobileNetV3-Small on identical inputs (paired difference $+0.066$, 95\% bootstrap CI $[+0.033,+0.097]$, 391 patients), and matched ablations show that foveation, the learned gate and the disagreement loss each change tune loss by at most 0.015. The audits are the durable contribution: temperature scaling removes over-confidence, entropy-based referral lowers accepted-case risk, evidence deletion confirms the model relies on the labelled raw-EEG window, and the compact model is three times less sensitive than the pretrained network to a change of STFT resolution. The entire study consumed 0.72 GPU-hours, and code, tests, executed notebooks and twenty provenance-tracked figures are released.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Intensive-care EEG is read for seizures and for the rhythmic and periodic patterns that surround them: lateralised and generalised periodic discharges (LPD, GPD) and lateralised and generalised rhythmic delta activity (LRDA, GRDA). Even expert readers disagree about these patterns, so the largest public dataset of such annotations, HMS Harmful Brain Activity Classification, scores models against the \emph{vote distribution} of up to 28 experts with the Kullback--Leibler divergence rather than against a single label \cite{hms2024,jing2023}. This changes what a good model is: it must be calibrated, it must know when to defer, and its evidence use must be auditable, because the ``ground truth'' is itself a disagreement.

Systems that would run such models next to a bedside monitor are constrained in a way that leaderboard solutions are not. Memory and storage are bounded, one small accelerator is shared, and every trained artefact has to be reproducible and inspectable. Two ideas from recent computer vision appear well suited to this regime. Foveated representations spend resolution where the label lives \cite{akbas2017foveated}, and economical multi-scale fusion \cite{kang2026dpnext} combines views without a large backbone. At the same time, recent EEG work warns that preprocessing choices silently move predictions \cite{hou2026samebrain}, that foundation models are not automatically better than task-matched controls \cite{kontras2026neuroatlas}, that purported evidence should be tested by deletion \cite{wang2026evidence}, and that robustness must be measured under declared perturbations \cite{mohammadi2026robustseiz}.

This paper asks a narrow, falsifiable question. \emph{At a fixed cache byte budget and a bounded single-device training budget, does preserving the labelled temporal centre and learning how to combine local EEG-derived spectra with longer spectral context improve prediction of expert votes on unseen patients, relative to a small pretrained convolutional network that sees exactly the same bytes?} We answer it with a study that was specified before the data were touched and that reports its negative result together with the audits that make the answer useful.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A pre-registered, leak-free protocol} for expert-vote EEG classification: connected-component patient/recording split, a 6\,GB active-cache cap with masks and metadata counted, at most eight named development configurations, a written protocol lock, three fixed seeds averaged as losses, and a single test evaluation with patient-cluster bootstrap inference \cite{nosek2018preregistration,efron1979bootstrap}.
\item \textbf{CAPE-EEG}, a 164k-parameter two-view model with byte-matched temporal foveation, a learned inspectable view gate and an annotation-disagreement auxiliary head, together with its exact byte- and exposure-matched controls.
\item \textbf{A resolved negative result.} The candidate is inferior to MobileNetV3-Small on identical inputs by $+0.066$ patient-mean KL (CI wholly above zero); none of the three mechanisms helps in matched ablations; a multi-scale fusion variant hurts.
\item \textbf{Audits that survive the negative result:} calibration, referral, disagreement prediction, sixteen prespecified subgroups, a six-condition corruption suite, evidence-deletion tests and measured latency, all with patient-cluster intervals.
\item \textbf{An open, tested release} (155 synthetic tests, seven executed notebooks, twenty figures with provenance sidecars) at \repourl.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{HMS and vision-inspired classifiers.} Jing et al.\ \cite{jing2023} showed that a deep model can reach expert-level agreement on the six-pattern task; the public HMS release \cite{hms2024} operationalised it as vote-distribution prediction scored by KL. VIPEEGNet \cite{sun2025vipeegnet} reports strong KL with an ImageNet-pretrained backbone on spectrogram images; its cohort and split differ from ours and its scores are therefore not comparable. Our comparator B3 is the smallest member of that family, MobileNetV3-Small \cite{howard2019mobilenetv3}, chosen so that the comparison is about representation rather than scale.

\textbf{Compact architectures and fusion.} Depthwise-separable convolutions \cite{howard2017mobilenets} and Group Normalisation \cite{wu2018groupnorm} make sub-200k-parameter trunks trainable at small batch sizes. DPNeXt \cite{kang2026dpnext} shows that inexpensive multi-scale fusion can replace heavy decoders for dense prediction; we borrow only the principle, as an exploratory block, and find it harmful here.

\textbf{Soft labels, calibration and deferral.} Learning from the full distribution of human judgements improves robustness and calibration \cite{peterson2019human}; temperature scaling \cite{guo2017calibration} is the minimal post-hoc calibrator we allow, fitted on a partition that never trains weights. Selective classification \cite{geifman2017selective} motivates the risk--coverage analysis in which a frozen input-only score decides which windows to refer.

\textbf{Reliability of EEG decoding.} The 2026 preprocessing-sensitivity, benchmark, evidence-bottleneck and robustness studies \cite{hou2026samebrain,kontras2026neuroatlas,wang2026evidence,mohammadi2026robustseiz} motivate, respectively, our declared STFT-resolution condition, our task-matched controls, our deletion audits and our corruption suite. None is reproduced; each is a reason a specific test exists.

\section{Data and Task}
\label{sec:data}
The source is the original HMS training release: 106,800 labelled windows from 1,950 patients, 17,089 EEG recordings (200\,Hz, 19 scalp electrodes and one EKG channel, 50\,s to 56\,min long) and 11,138 spectrograms (2\,s rows, four regions $\times$ 100 frequencies from 0.59 to 19.92\,Hz). Each row gives an EEG offset $e$ and a spectrogram offset $s$: the labelled centre is seconds $[e{+}20,e{+}30)$ of the recording and $[s{+}295,s{+}305)$ of the spectrogram, and the two clocks are not the same \cite{hms2024}. All offsets lie on the sample grid. Votes $v_{ik}\ge 0$ over the six classes have total $n_i\in[1,28]$; 4,360 windows carry a single vote and 5,248 have tied maxima. The target is $q_{ik}=v_{ik}/n_i$.

\section{CAPE-EEG}
\subsection{Byte-budgeted two-view evidence}
\label{sec:cache}
Every window is reduced to two float16 tensors. The \textbf{local view} $L\in\mathbb{R}^{4\times 64\times 32}$ comes from the 50\,s raw window: sixteen bipolar leads in four regional chains (LL, RL, LP, RP), per-lead median removal, a Hann STFT (256-sample window, hop 64, no boundary padding, 153 frames), linear power averaged over valid leads of a region, overlap-weighted integration into 64 linear frequency bins over 0.5--40\,Hz and 32 time bins, and a final logarithm. In the \emph{foveated} encoding the time bins cover $[0,20)$\,s with 8 bins, the labelled centre $[20,30)$\,s with 16 bins and $[30,50)$\,s with 8 bins; the \emph{uniform} control uses 32 equal bins. Both cost exactly the same bytes. The \textbf{context view} $C\in\mathbb{R}^{4\times 64\times 64}$ integrates the supplied 600\,s spectrogram interval onto 64 frequency and 64 time bins per region. Each cell carries a validity bit: a frame needs at least 90\% observed samples and at least two valid leads, a bin needs at least half of its weight from valid frames, and non-finite supplied cells are invalid. Truncated edges are masked, never shifted.

One row therefore occupies $4\times 64\times 96\times 2 = 49{,}152$ bytes of values and 3,072 bytes of masks. The realised cache holds 106,800 rows in 5.582\,GB, below the 6\,GB cap, plus a 1.859\,GB development-only uniform alternate; float16 storage changes log-power by at most 0.0076. Conversion took 786\,s with 16 CPU workers whose resident memory never exceeded 0.77\,GiB. Figure~\ref{fig:align} shows the alignment audit that every cache had to pass: a synthetic 3\,Hz burst confined to seconds 22--28 must land in foveated columns 8--23 and in the 3\,Hz bin.

Figure~\ref{fig:arch} shows the candidate. Each view has its own $3\times 3$ stem (4$\to$24 channels) and both share a depthwise-separable trunk with 24, 48, 64, 96 and 128 channels, frequency/time strides of (2,1), (2,2), (2,2) and (1,1), and two residual 128-channel blocks; GroupNorm avoids batch statistics. Local pooling concatenates the global mean, the global maximum and a centre-region mean whose column weights are the overlap of each bin with $[20,30)$\,s, so that the uniform control pools its \emph{true} centre rather than columns 8--23. Context pooling uses mean and maximum. Linear projections give $u_L,u_C\in\mathbb{R}^{128}$ and independent heads give $p_L$ and $p_C$. The gate $g$ sees the two embeddings, the two valid fractions and the Jensen--Shannon divergence between $p_L$ and $p_C$; no identifier, vote count or label enters the network at inference. An unavailable view forces $g$ to the surviving path. Heads, gate and mixture run in float32 even under bf16 autocast.

\subsection{Losses}
For window $i$ the classification loss is the soft cross-entropy $-\sum_k q_{ik}\log p_{ik}$ on the mixture, whose optimum equals that of $\kl(q_i\|p_i)$. Observed pairwise annotator disagreement $d_i = 1-\sum_k v_{ik}(v_{ik}-1)/[n_i(n_i-1)]$ is defined only for $n_i>1$ and is not clipped to its population maximum of $5/6$. The total loss is $\overline{\mathrm{CE}} + 0.1\cdot\overline{(\hat d_i - d_i)^2}_{\,n_i>1}$, with a differentiable zero when a mini-batch has no multi-annotator window. No class weighting, vote weighting, focal loss or label sharpening is used.

\subsection{Baselines and named configurations}
\label{sec:configs}
B0 is the training-set vote prior; B1 is an L2-regularised multinomial logistic regression on 74 fixed band-power features fitted to fractional targets on the CPU. B2 is the byte-matched conventional control: the same trunk on the uniform local encoding with a fixed 50:50 mixture and no auxiliary head (147,524 parameters). A1 adds foveation, A2 adds the learned gate, and P adds the auxiliary loss. B3 is MobileNetV3-Small \cite{howard2019mobilenetv3,wightman2019timm} with four input channels and six outputs, fed the two normalised views concatenated along time (64$\times$96) and bilinearly resized to 128$\times$192 (1,524,150 parameters). P+MSF is an exploratory variant whose last residual block is replaced by an economical multi-scale fusion block (parallel depthwise convolutions with temporal dilations 1/2/3, a $1\times 1$ fusion and a channel gate; 208,070 parameters). Only B2 and B3 may become the comparator, and the choice is made on tune data.

\subsection{Training}
All configurations use AdamW \cite{loshchilov2019adamw} (weight decay 0.01), learning rate $10^{-3}$ for compact models and $10^{-4}$/$10^{-3}$ for the pretrained backbone/head with one frozen-backbone epoch, cosine decay with 200 warm-up steps, batch 64, gradient clipping at 1.0, bf16 autocast after a parity test (maximum $|\Delta p|=0.005$), and mild augmentation only: Gaussian gain jitter, frequency masking of at most four bins, and single-region dropout with probability 0.1. Normalisation statistics (per view and region, median and scaled MAD of valid cells) are fitted on the training partition only, refitted on train+tune for the final phase; invalid cells are zeroed \emph{after} normalisation.

Figure~\ref{fig:protocol} summarises the study as it ran. The development phase may fit at most eight named configurations on the training partition and select on tune; the only permitted optimiser pilots are a learning-rate multiplier of 0.3 and a common shorter epoch count. The lock then freezes the comparator (the better of B2 and B3 on tune), the final schedule, the referral score (the lowest area under the risk--coverage curve on tune among predictive entropy, one-minus-maximum-probability and $\hat d$), target-entropy tertiles, sixteen subgroups, the six corruption conditions and the figure list. Final models are refit on train+tune for the frozen number of epochs with seeds 101, 202 and 303; seed 101 is the deployment exemplar chosen in advance. A scalar temperature is fitted on Calibration-T by patient-weighted cross-entropy, referral thresholds for 80/90/95\% acceptance are fixed on Calibration-P, and the test partition is read once.

The primary estimand is $\Delta = \bar K_P - \bar K_B$, where $\bar K_m$ is the mean over patients of the patient-mean $\kl(q\|p)$ (natural log, $p$ clipped at $10^{-7}$ and renormalised), averaged over the three single-model seeds---an algorithm comparison, not an ensemble. Superiority requires the 95\% percentile interval of 2,000 paired patient-bootstrap replicates to lie wholly below zero; an interval crossing zero is inconclusive. Secondary outcomes are row-weighted KL, soft cross-entropy, soft squared error, expected categorical Brier score, hard-label metrics on the 20,482 unique-majority windows (823 ties excluded), soft and majority top-label expected calibration error with ten equal-count bins and a twenty-group merge rule, disagreement MSE and Spearman correlation by vote stratum, risk--coverage curves, six corruption conditions on a label-blind subset of 777 test windows from all 391 patients, view/region deletion and ranked-versus-random occlusion on 48 prespecified cases, and measured latency and memory. All GPU wall time, including smoke tests, is charged to a 12-hour ledger.

\subsection{Calibration, referral and disagreement}
Both architectures are over-confident when trained with soft cross-entropy: temperatures fitted on Calibration-T range from 1.21 to 1.39 for P and from 1.28 to 1.33 for B3 (Table~\ref{tab:seeds}), none at a bound, and scaling cuts soft top-label ECE from 0.10--0.14 to 0.03--0.06 and patient-mean KL by 0.046--0.081 for every model and seed (Figure~\ref{fig:calib}). With entropy as the frozen referral score and thresholds fixed on Calibration-P, referring 11.8\% of test windows lowers P's accepted-case patient KL from 0.923 to 0.911 (seed 101); for B3, referring 9.1\% lowers it from 0.878 to 0.874. Area under the risk--coverage curve over coverage 0.5--1.0 is 0.401--0.415 for P and 0.365--0.372 for B3 across seeds. The disagreement head predicts observed pairwise annotator disagreement with Spearman 0.31, 0.26 and 0.25 across seeds (MSE 0.088--0.094 on 20,795 estimable windows) and 0.23--0.24 within each vote-count stratum; as a referral score it is worse than entropy (AURC 0.444--0.448). \section{Implementation and Reproducibility}
\label{sec:impl}
The study is engineered so that a reviewer can rebuild every number from the public release and an authorised copy of the source data. \textbf{Cache conversion} groups label windows by recording, reads each Parquet file once, writes float16 values and packed masks into pre-allocated memory-mapped shards of 2,048 rows through \texttt{.partial} files that are renamed atomically only after both views exist for every row, and checksums each shard into a manifest. Sixteen worker processes converted all 106,800 rows in 786\,s; pinning BLAS to one thread per worker was necessary, because the default multi-threaded matrix products made the context pass two orders of magnitude slower. \textbf{Training} uses a supervisor that samples CUDA allocation, process-tree resident memory and system availability every fifty steps and aborts with an \texttt{INCOMPLETE} status at declared stops (12\,GiB, 32\,GiB, 32\,GiB free); a \texttt{flock}-based GPU lock forbids concurrent jobs, and every job appends its wall time to the ledger whether it passed or failed. \textbf{Roles} are enforced in code: a development-role loader raises on any request for the test partition, and the evaluator role logs each test access against the protocol hash. \textbf{Release} is an allowlisted export with identifier and secret scans of the working tree and of every reachable Git object; notebooks are published with aggregate-only outputs, per-row predictions and weights stay private, and public tables suppress cells with fewer than ten patients. The repository ships 155 synthetic CPU tests (contracts, alignment, spectral encoders, splits, cache round trip, model, losses, engine, metrics, bootstrap, calibration, figures), seven executed notebooks and twenty figures whose sidecars record the hashes and run identifiers they were drawn from.

\section{Discussion}
\textbf{Why the compact candidate lost.} Three observations explain the result. First, the task is patient-limited: 1,170 training patients contribute 64k highly correlated windows, and every architecture overfits after two or three epochs. Under the frozen short schedule the candidate ties the comparator on tune but not on the larger locked test, and its seed spread is 60\% larger. Second, ImageNet features transfer well to the upsampled four-channel log-spectrogram image; the comparator's advantage is largest exactly where the evidence is clearest (unanimous or heavily annotated windows, LPD), which is where transferred texture detectors help most. Third, the three mechanisms address problems the data do not pose: 32 uniform bins over 50\,s already resolve the 10-second centre, the two views mostly agree so the gate re-weights similar distributions, and the disagreement target is too noisy (Spearman 0.3) to regularise the classifier. The economical multi-scale block simply adds capacity that overfits faster.

\textbf{What the candidate is good for.} It is nine times smaller, has lower batch-1 latency, is statistically indistinguishable from the comparator on the most ambiguous windows and on LRDA, and is markedly more stable to a change of spectral preprocessing. For a bedside system whose acquisition pipeline may drift, that last property is a systems argument in its favour that the headline KL hides.

\textbf{Implications for next-generation clinical computing.} The study is an existence proof that a complete, auditable evaluation of an EEG model---leak-free split, byte-capped evidence, locked protocol, calibration, deferral, corruption suite, evidence deletion, latency---fits in under one GPU-hour on a single desktop accelerator. Pre-registration cost nothing in compute and prevented the two most common ways a positive result could have been manufactured here: choosing the comparator or the epoch after seeing the test set, and reporting the seed that happened to win. The same machinery supports model governance: hashes bind every table and figure to a split, a cache and a protocol; per-row artefacts never leave the private tier; and the release contains no patient data, no identifiers and no weights.

\textbf{Threats to validity.} The comparator was selected on tune from only two candidates, so a stronger small pretrained network might widen the gap further, not narrow it. The mechanism ablations are single-seed pilots whose effects are smaller than the seed spread observed in the final phase, which is why they are reported without intervals and why no mechanism claim is made in either direction. Temperature scaling and referral thresholds were fitted on partitions of 95 and 98 patients, so the calibration figures have wide bands. Finally, the test partition was read once, but the pipeline bug fixed between the first and second evaluation attempts (a renamed dictionary key) occurred before any metric was computed and is recorded in the test-access log.

\textbf{Design guidance for practitioners.} Five lessons generalise beyond this dataset. (1) Cap and count the evidence: a byte budget that includes validity masks and metadata makes ``compact'' a measurable property rather than a parameter count, and it made the uniform and foveated encodings exactly comparable. (2) Freeze the schedule, not the checkpoint: when every model peaks within three epochs, selecting checkpoints on a tuning set overstates what a complete refit will deliver, and a short annealed schedule recovered 0.056 KL for the candidate. (3) Choose the comparator on data, not on convenience: the smallest pretrained network available was stronger than every compact design and would have been the wrong control to omit. (4) Calibrate on a partition that never trains weights and report the temperature: values of 1.2--1.4 are a diagnostic of soft-label over-confidence that the KL alone hides. (5) Audit evidence by deletion and preprocessing by perturbation; the two audits disagreed about which model is safer, and a deployment decision needs both.

\textbf{Data governance.} The release contains no EEG samples, spectrogram values, identifiers, split membership tables, per-window predictions or trained weights; each is reconstructible by an authorised holder of the source data from the published hashes and code. Public tables suppress any cell with fewer than ten patients, and the executed notebooks were scanned for identifiers before export. Secret scanning of the working tree and of every reachable Git object ran before the repository was pushed. These are data-minimisation measures, not an anonymisation guarantee, and the source data remain under the competition's terms.

\textbf{Limitations.} Single dataset and site; segment labels only, so no event-level sensitivity, onset latency or false alarms per hour; the disagreement statistic assumes exchangeable annotators; the corruption conditions are representation-level perturbations, not validated artefact simulators; single-annotator windows have unavoidably noisy targets; the mechanism ablations are single-seed pilots; and weights are withheld pending a rights review. The natural next hypotheses within the same protocol are patient-level regularisation (fewer correlated windows per patient per epoch) and self-supervised pre-training of the compact trunk on the unlabelled portions of the 17,300 recordings; both would be new named configurations under a new lock.

\section{Conclusion}
We asked whether byte-matched temporal foveation, a learned view gate and an annotation-disagreement auxiliary target let a 164k-parameter model match a small pretrained CNN on expert-vote EEG classification, and we specified how the question would be answered before touching the data. The answer is no: the candidate is inferior by 0.066 patient-mean KL with an interval wholly above zero, and none of the mechanisms helps in matched ablations. The audits nevertheless yield transferable findings---over-confidence that temperature scaling repairs, entropy-based deferral that lowers accepted-case risk, evidence use that survives deletion tests, and a pretrained network that is three times more fragile to spectral preprocessing than the compact one---and the protocol yields a cheap, reproducible benchmark for the next attempt. Code, tests, executed notebooks, twenty provenance-tracked figures and every aggregate table are available at \repourl.
